## 1. Keep doing it, or do it again

<GrammarBox client:load pattern="V-ます stem + 続ける" meaning="Continue doing V." formation="Remove ます from the polite form and add 続ける." examples={[
  { japanese: "毎日漢字を覚え続けています。", romaji: "mainichi kanji o oboe tsuzukete imasu.", translation: "I continue memorizing kanji every day." },
  { japanese: "雨が降り続いたので、試合は中止になった。", romaji: "ame ga furitsuzuita node, shiai wa chuushi ni natta.", translation: "Because the rain kept falling, the match was cancelled." },
  { japanese: "難しくても、話し続けてみてください。", romaji: "muzukashikute mo, hanashitsuzukete mite kudasai.", translation: "Even if it is difficult, please keep trying to speak." }
]} />

`繰り返す` means to repeat an action or event, often deliberately: `同じ発音を繰り返す` (repeat the same pronunciation). It does not necessarily mean the action is continuous without stopping.

<CommonMistake title="続ける is not simply 'many times'">
  <p>Use `〜続ける` for sustained continuation. Use `繰り返す` when the same action happens again and again. A learner can `練習を続ける` over months and `同じ文を繰り返す` during one exercise.</p>
</CommonMistake>

## 2. Every time, or at every interval

<ParticleCompare client:load title="〜たびに and 〜ごとに" items={[
  { label: "V-るたびに", role: "each time an event happens", example: "この歌を聞くたびに、旅行を思い出す。", note: "Every time I hear this song, I remember the trip." },
  { label: "N のたびに", role: "on each occasion", example: "試験のたびに、緊張する。", note: "I get nervous at every exam." },
  { label: "N ごとに", role: "at a regular interval or for each unit", example: "一週間ごとに部屋を掃除する。", note: "I clean the room every week." },
  { label: "N ごとに", role: "each item in a set", example: "人ごとに考え方が違う。", note: "Each person thinks differently." }
]} />

`〜たびに` fits a recurring event and its recurring result. `〜ごとに` is especially natural with measurable units such as days, pages, stations, or groups.

## 3. Changes that move together

<GrammarBox client:load pattern="N に つれて / V-る に つれて" meaning="As N changes, another change happens gradually along with it." formation="Noun + につれて; dictionary verb + につれて." examples={[
  { japanese: "日本語を勉強するにつれて、聞き取れる言葉が増えてきた。", romaji: "nihongo o benkyou suru ni tsurete, kikitoreru kotoba ga fuete kita.", translation: "As I study Japanese, the words I can catch have gradually increased." },
  { japanese: "季節が変わるにつれて、日が短くなる。", romaji: "kisetsu ga kawaru ni tsurete, hi ga mijikaku naru.", translation: "As the season changes, the days become shorter." }
]} />

<GrammarBox client:load pattern="N に したがって / V-る に したがって" meaning="As; in accordance with. A formal expression for a gradual change or following a rule." formation="Noun + にしたがって; dictionary verb + にしたがって." examples={[
  { japanese: "年齢が上がるにしたがって、責任も大きくなる。", romaji: "nenrei ga agaru ni shitagatte, sekinin mo ookiku naru.", translation: "As people get older, their responsibilities also increase." },
  { japanese: "案内にしたがって、こちらにお並びください。", romaji: "annai ni shitagatte, kochira ni onarabi kudasai.", translation: "Please line up here according to the instructions." }
]} />

`〜につれて` is common in everyday descriptions of natural trends. `〜にしたがって` is slightly more formal and can also mean “following” an instruction, rule, or plan.

<CultureNote title="Progress is often described as a process" ja="継続">
  <p>In study, work, and training contexts, Japanese speakers frequently value steady continuation: <strong>続けることが大切です</strong> (“Continuing is important”). Expressions such as <strong>少しずつ</strong>, <strong>徐々に</strong>, and <strong>〜につれて</strong> help describe progress without claiming instant results.</p>
</CultureNote>

## 5. Guided practice and reading

1. “Keep reading the article.”
2. “Every time I visit Kyoto, I discover something new.”
3. “We have a meeting every two weeks.”
4. “As the number of users increases, the service becomes more convenient.”
5. “Please follow the instructions.”

> 私は発音をよくするために、短い会話を毎日繰り返して練習しています。最初は録音を聞くたびに自分の間違いが気になりました。しかし、練習を続けるにつれて、少しずつ自然に話せるようになってきました。今は一週間ごとに録音を比べて、次に直したい点を決めています。

**Reading check:** Find the repeated action, the emotional reaction that happens every time, the gradual change, and the regular interval.

Translation: “To improve my pronunciation, I practice by repeating short conversations every day. At first, every time I listened to a recording, I worried about my mistakes. However, as I continued practicing, I gradually became able to speak more naturally. Now I compare recordings every week and decide what I want to improve next.”
