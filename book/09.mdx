<SectionIntro level="N5">
Now that you can tell time and introduce yourself, it's time to move! In Japanese, we use different particles depending on whether a place is a **destination** we are heading toward, or a **location** where we are performing an activity. We also use particles to explain *how* we get there. Let's explore the world of places and movement!
</SectionIntro>

## 1. Destination: [Place] ＋ へ (e)

When you are moving toward a place (going, coming, or returning), you mark that destination with **へ**.

> [!CAUTION]
> The Hiragana character **へ (he)** is pronounced **"e"** when it is used as a particle.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Place] ＋ へ ＋ [Movement Verb]"
  meaning="Going toward a destination."
  formation="Noun (Place) + へ + いきます / きます / かえります"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "がっこう へ いきます。", romaji: "Gakkou e ikimasu.", translation: "I go to school." },
    { japanese: "にほん へ きました。", romaji: "Nihon e kimashita.", translation: "I came to Japan." },
    { japanese: "うち へ かえります。", romaji: "Uchi e kaerimasu.", translation: "I am going home (returning home)." }
  ]}
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="どこ へ いきます か。"
  romaji="Doko e ikimasu ka."
  translation="Where are you going?"
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="あした、 だいがく へ いきます。"
  romaji="Ashita, daigaku e ikimasu."
  translation="Tomorrow, I will go to the university."
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="スーパー へ いきます。"
  romaji="Suupaa e ikimasu."
  translation="I am going to the supermarket."

## 2. Location of Action: [Place] ＋ で (de)

When you perform an action *at* a place (reading, eating, studying, etc.), you mark that place with **で**.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Place] ＋ で ＋ [Action Verb]"
  meaning="Performing an action at a specific location."
  formation="Noun (Place) + で + Verb"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "としょかん で ほん を よみます。", romaji: "Toshokan de hon o yomimasu.", translation: "I read books at the library." },
    { japanese: "レストラン で ごはん を たべます。", romaji: "Resutoran de gohan o tabemasu.", translation: "I eat a meal at a restaurant." },
    { japanese: "うち で にほんご を べんきょうします。", romaji: "Uchi de Nihongo o benkyoushimasu.", translation: "I study Japanese at home." }
  ]}
/>

> [!TIP]
> **へ vs. で:**
> - Use **へ** for *movement* toward a goal (going to).
> - Use **で** for *activity* happening in a spot (doing at).

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="どこ で べんきょうします か。"
  romaji="Doko de benkyoushimasu ka."
  translation="Where do you study?"
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="デパート で かばん を かいました。"
  romaji="Depaato de kaban o kaimashita."
  translation="I bought a bag at the department store."
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="あした、 こうえん で あいましょう。"
  romaji="Ashita, kouen de aimashou."
  translation="Let's meet at the park tomorrow."
/>

## 3. Means of Transport: [Vehicle] ＋ で (de)

The particle **で** has another job! It also indicates the "means" or "tool" you use to do something. When followed by a movement verb, it tells us *how* you are traveling.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Vehicle] ＋ で ＋ いきます"
  meaning="Going by [means of transport]."
  formation="Noun (Vehicle) + で + Movement Verb"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "でんしゃ で いきます。", romaji: "Densha de ikimasu.", translation: "I go by train." },
    { japanese: "くるま で うち へ かえります。", romaji: "Kuruma de uchi e kaerimasu.", translation: "I return home by car." },
    { japanese: "じてんしゃ で きました。", romaji: "Jitensha de kimashita.", translation: "I came by bicycle." }
  ]}
/>

> [!NOTE]
> **Walking:** If you go on foot, you say **あるいて (aruite)**. You do NOT use the particle **で** here because "walking" is a form of the verb itself, not a separate tool/vehicle.

## 4. Cultural Note: Train Culture & IC Cards (電車)

Trains are the lifeblood of Japan. The system is incredibly efficient but can be overwhelming for beginners.
- **Quiet Zones:** Trains are generally quiet. Talking on the phone is strictly prohibited, and loud conversations are discouraged.
- **IC Cards (Suica/Pasmo):** Instead of buying paper tickets, most people use rechargeable IC cards. You just tap them on the gate when you enter and exit. Today, these can even be stored on your smartphone.
- **Priority Seats:** Look for the "Priority Seat" (*Yuusen-seki*) signs. These are reserved for the elderly, injured, pregnant, or those with disabilities. It is polite to offer your seat if someone in need is standing.
- **Rush Hour:** Be prepared for "Kasshu" (crush) during morning and evening commutes in big cities like Tokyo!
