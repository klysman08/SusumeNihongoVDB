## 1. Subjective Reason: 〜から

`〜から` attaches directly to the end of a sentence. It places the focus heavily on the **speaker's personal opinion, will, or subjective judgment**. Because it is subjective, it naturally pairs with commands, requests, or personal intentions.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Sentence 1] から、[Sentence 2]。"
  meaning="Because [Sentence 1] (subjective reason), [Sentence 2]."
  formation="Plain/Polite Form + から"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "危ないから、触らないでください。", romaji: "abunai kara, sawaranaide kudasai.", translation: "Because it is dangerous, please do not touch it." },
    { japanese: "明日テストがあるから、今夜は勉強します。", romaji: "ashita tesuto ga aru kara, konya wa benkyou shimasu.", translation: "Because there's a test tomorrow, I will study tonight." },
    { japanese: "美味しいから、たくさん食べてね。", romaji: "oishii kara, takusan tabete ne.", translation: "Because it's delicious, eat a lot, okay?" }
  ]}
/>

## 2. Objective & Polite Reason: 〜ので

`〜ので` is the polite, objective counterpart to `〜から`. It presents the reason as a natural sequence of events or a universally understood fact. It is the preferred way to state reasons in **business, formal requests, and apologies**.

**Formation Rules:**
* **Verbs & i-Adjectives:** Plain form + ので
* **Na-Adjectives & Nouns:** Add `な` before ので (e.g., 静かなので、雨なので)

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Sentence 1] ので、[Sentence 2]。"
  meaning="Since/Because [Sentence 1] (objective reason), [Sentence 2]."
  formation="Plain Form + ので (Noun/Na-Adj + なので)"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "お腹が痛いので、先に帰らせていただきます。", romaji: "onaka ga itai node, saki ni kaerasete itadakimasu.", translation: "Since my stomach hurts, I will (humbly) go home first." },
    { japanese: "バスが遅れたので、会議に遅刻しました。", romaji: "basu ga okureta node, kaigi ni chikoku shimashita.", translation: "Since the bus was delayed, I was late for the meeting." },
    { japanese: "今日は日曜日なので、銀行は休みです。", romaji: "kyou wa nichiyoubi na node, ginkou wa yasumi desu.", translation: "Since today is Sunday, the bank is closed." }
  ]}
/>

> [!WARNING]
> Because `〜ので` is an objective statement of fact, you generally **cannot** follow it with a strong personal command like `〜しなさい`. Use `〜から` for direct commands!

## 3. Light Cause/Emotional Trigger: て-form / で

You can use the `て-form` of a verb or adjective (or `で` for nouns) to indicate a natural, light cause. This is used when the result is an **uncontrollable emotion** or a **natural state**.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[A て-form]、[B]。"
  meaning="Because of A, B naturally happened/was felt."
  formation="Verb-te / Adj-kute / Noun-de"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "ニュースを聞いて、びっくりしました。", romaji: "nyuusu o kiite, bikkuri shimashita.", translation: "I heard the news and was surprised." },
    { japanese: "試験が難しくて、わかりませんでした。", romaji: "shiken ga muzukashikute, wakarimasen deshita.", translation: "The exam was difficult, so I didn't understand it." },
    { japanese: "風邪で、学校を休みました。", romaji: "kaze de, gakkou o yasumimashita.", translation: "Because of a cold, I was absent from school." }
  ]}
/>

## 4. Formal Cause & Purpose: 〜ために

`〜ために` is formal and has two meanings depending on the context:

### A. Formal Cause (Due to)
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="大雨のために、電車が止まっています。"
  romaji="ooame no tame ni, densha ga tomatte imasu."
  translation="Due to heavy rain, the train is stopped."
/>

### B. Purpose (In order to)
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="留学するために、お金を貯めています。"
  romaji="ryuugaku suru tame ni, okane o tamete imasu."
  translation="In order to study abroad, I am saving money."
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="健康のために、毎日走っています。"
  romaji="kenkou no tame ni, mainichi hashitte imasu."
  translation="For the sake of health, I run every day."
/>

---

## Cultural Insight: The Art of the Japanese Excuse

In Japanese culture, providing a reason is not just about giving information; it's about maintaining social harmony (**Wa**). Using `〜から` for an excuse can sound like you are trying to justify your actions or blame external factors. 

By contrast, using `〜ので` suggests that the situation was a natural occurrence beyond your control, which sounds humbler and more apologetic. This is why in service industries or workplace apologies, you will almost exclusively hear `〜ので`. Even if you are late because you overslept, saying `寝坊したので` (Since I overslept) sounds softer than `寝坊したから`.
