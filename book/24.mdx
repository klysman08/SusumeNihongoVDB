## 1. Trying Something: 〜てみる

When you do something as an experiment to see what it is like, use `〜てみる`. This comes from the verb `見る` (to see), but when used as an auxiliary, it is usually written in hiragana (`みる`).

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="Verb (て-form) + みる"
  meaning="To try doing [verb] to see what happens / what it's like."
  formation="食べる → 食べてみる (Try eating)"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "このケーキを食べてみてください。", romaji: "kono keeki o tabete mite kudasai.", translation: "Please try eating this cake." },
    { japanese: "あの新しいレストランへ行ってみます。", romaji: "ano atarashii resutoran e itte mimasu.", translation: "I am going to try going to that new restaurant." },
    { japanese: "サイズが合うか、着てみました。", romaji: "saizu ga au ka, kite mimashita.", translation: "I tried wearing it to see if the size fits." }
  ]}
/>

> [!NOTE]
> `〜てみる` conjugates just like a normal Group 2 (ru-verb). You can say `てみます`, `てみない`, `てみたい` (want to try doing), etc.

## 2. Regret or Completion: 〜てしまう

The extension `〜てしまう` has two distinct meanings depending on the context:
1. **Regret/Oops:** Doing something accidentally or unfortunately.
2. **Completion:** Finishing an action completely and thoroughly.

In casual speech, `〜てしまう` contracts to `〜ちゃう`, and `〜でしまう` contracts to `〜じゃう`.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="Verb (て-form) + しまう"
  meaning="To do [verb] completely, OR to do [verb] accidentally / with regret."
  formation="忘れる → 忘れてしまう (Forget completely / Unfortunately forget)"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "ごめんなさい、パスポートを忘れてしまいました。", romaji: "gomennasai, pasupooto o wasurete shimaimashita.", translation: "I'm sorry, I accidentally forgot my passport." },
    { japanese: "ビールを全部飲んでしまいました。", romaji: "biiru o zenbu nonde shimaimashita.", translation: "I completely drank all the beer." },
    { japanese: "あ！ケーキ食べちゃった！", romaji: "a! keeki tabechatta!", translation: "Ah! I accidentally ate the cake! (Casual)" }
  ]}
/>

## 3. Preparation: 〜ておく

When you do an action *in preparation* for the future, use `〜ておく`. It implies leaving something in a certain state so that it is ready later. In casual speech, it contracts to `〜とく`.

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="Verb (て-form) + おく"
  meaning="To do [verb] in advance / as preparation."
  formation="買う → 買っておく (Buy in advance)"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "パーティーの前に、飲み物を買っておきます。", romaji: "paatii no mae ni, nomimono o katte okimasu.", translation: "Before the party, I will buy drinks in advance." },
    { japanese: "ホテルを予約しておきました。", romaji: "hoteru o yoyaku shite okimashita.", translation: "I booked the hotel in advance." },
    { japanese: "ドアを開けておいてください。", romaji: "doa o akete oite kudasai.", translation: "Please leave the door open (for future convenience)." }
  ]}
/>

### Cultural Insight: The Concept of Dandori (段取り)

Japanese culture places a high value on **Dandori (段取り)**, which refers to "preparation and planning." Using `〜ておく` reflects this cultural mindset. Whether it's setting up a meeting room 15 minutes early or buying a gift weeks before a birthday, performing actions *in advance* is seen as a sign of consideration for others and professional competence.

## 4. Movement in Time/Space: 〜てくる and 〜ていく

These two extensions describe the *direction* of an action from the speaker's perspective, either physically or temporally.

### A. 〜てくる (Toward the speaker / Up to now)
1. **Physical:** Do an action and then come back (`行ってきます`).
2. **Temporal:** A change that has been happening up until now, or something starting to happen (`雨が降ってきた`).

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="ちょっとコンビニへ行ってきます。"
  romaji="chotto konbini e itte kimasu."
  translation="I'm going to the convenience store and coming right back."
/>
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="急に雨が降ってきました。"
  romaji="kyuu ni ame ga futte kimashita."
  translation="It suddenly started raining (the rain came toward us in time)."
/>
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="日本語が少しずつわかってきました。"
  romaji="nihongo ga sukoshizutsu wakatte kimashita."
  translation="I've gradually started to understand Japanese (up until now)."
/>

### B. 〜ていく (Away from the speaker / From now on)
1. **Physical:** Do an action and move away from the current location.
2. **Temporal:** A change that will continue to happen from now into the future.

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="日本の人口は減っていくでしょう。"
  romaji="nihon no jinkou wa hette iku deshou."
  translation="The Japanese population will continue to decrease (into the future)."
/>
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="会社にお弁当を持っていきます。"
  romaji="kaisha ni obentou o motte ikimasu."
  translation="I will take a bento to the company (taking it away from current location)."
/>
<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="これからも日本語を勉強し続けていきます。"
  romaji="korekara mo nihongo o benkyou shitsudukete ikimasu."
  translation="I will continue to keep studying Japanese from now on."
/>
