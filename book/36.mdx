## 1. Obligation: "Must Do"

There are several ways to say "must do," but they all follow the same grammatical formula: take the **Nai-form** of a verb, drop the final `い`, and add a conditional suffix (`ければ` or `きゃ`) followed by `なりません` or `だめ`.

The most formal and standard written form is **〜なければなりません**.
*(Literally: If you do not do [verb], it will not become [okay]).*

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Nai-form minus 'i'] + なければなりません"
  meaning="Must do / Have to do [verb]."
  formation="Verb-nakereba narimasen"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "明日は早く起きなければなりません。", romaji: "ashita wa hayaku okinakereba narimasen.", translation: "Tomorrow I must wake up early." },
    { japanese: "毎日、薬を飲まなければなりません。", romaji: "mainichi, kusuri o nomanakereba narimasen.", translation: "I must take medicine every day." },
    { japanese: "試験の前に、もっと勉強しなければなりません。", romaji: "shiken no mae ni, motto benkyou shinakereba narimasen.", translation: "I must study more before the exam." }
  ]}
/>

### Casual Variations
In spoken Japanese, `〜なければなりません` is often shortened for speed and natural flow.

* **〜なきゃ(いけない/だめ):** The most common spoken casual form. 
* **〜なくちゃ(いけない/だめ):** Similar to なきゃ, but slightly more colloquial.
* **〜ないと(いけない/だめ):** Meaning "If I don't [do X], then [it's bad]."

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="もう遅いから、帰らなきゃ。"
  romaji="mou osoi kara, kaeranakya."
  translation="Because it's late already, I gotta go home."
/>

<ExampleSentence client:load

## 2. Lack of Obligation: "Don't Have To"

To say that an action is *unnecessary* ("you don't have to do it"), you use the negative te-form (`〜なくて`) followed by `もいいです`. 
*(Literally: Even if you do not do [verb], it is fine).*

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="[Nai-form minus 'i'] + なくてもいいです"
  meaning="Do not have to do / It is okay not to do [verb]."
  formation="Verb-nakute mo ii desu"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "明日は休みだから、早く起きなくてもいいです。", romaji: "ashita wa yasumi dakara, hayaku okinakute mo ii desu.", translation: "Tomorrow is a day off, so I don't have to wake up early." },
    { japanese: "嫌いな物は、食べなくてもいいですよ。", romaji: "kirai na mono wa, tabenakute mo ii desu yo.", translation: "You don't have to eat things you dislike." },
    { japanese: "急がなくてもいいです。時間は十分にあります。", romaji: "isoganakute mo ii desu. jikan wa juubun ni arimasu.", translation: "You don't have to hurry. There is plenty of time." }
  ]}
/>

## 3. Strong Advice: "Should" (〜べき)

When you want to express that something is logically, societally, or morally the *right thing to do*, use `〜べき`. This is much stronger than the advice form `〜ほうがいい`. 

**Formation:** Attach `べき` directly to the **Dictionary Form** of a verb. 
*(Special Case: する can become either するべき or すべき).*

<GrammarBox client:load 
  pattern="Dictionary Form + べき"
  meaning="Should do / Ought to do [verb] (Strong recommendation)."
  formation="Verb-Dict + べき"
  examples={[
    { japanese: "約束は守るべきだ。", romaji: "yakusoku wa mamoru beki da.", translation: "Promises should be kept." },
    { japanese: "学生はもっと勉強すべきです。", romaji: "gakusei wa motto benkyou subeki desu.", translation: "Students ought to study more." },
    { japanese: "お年寄りには親切にするべきです。", romaji: "otoshiyori ni wa shinsetsu ni suru beki desu.", translation: "One should be kind to the elderly." }
  ]}
/>

To say "should **not** do", keep the verb in the dictionary form and negate the べき: `〜べきではない`.

<ExampleSentence client:load 
  japanese="そんなひどいことを言うべきではありません。"
  romaji="sonna hidoi koto o iu beki dewa arimasen."
  translation="You should not say such terrible things."
/>

## Cultural Insight: Responsibility and "Giri"

In Japanese society, obligation is often framed through **Giri** (義理 - social obligation) and **Sekinin** (責任 - responsibility). While English speakers might say "I have to" as a personal requirement, Japanese speakers often use `〜なければならない` to express a duty to the group (family, company, or society). 

Failing to meet an obligation can cause **Meiwaku** (迷惑 - trouble/inconvenience) to others. This is why `〜べき` is so strong; it implies that failing to do the action violates the harmony of the group.
